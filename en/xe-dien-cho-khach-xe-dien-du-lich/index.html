Electric passenger vehicles, electric tourist vehicles for resorts and urban areas

Electric passenger vehicles, tourist electric vehicles, and golf carts for resorts, tourist areas, urban zones, and factories. ICD provides consultation on seating capacity, battery, and route planning.

Electric passenger vehicles and tourist electric vehicles are internal-area transport means that operate quietly and emission-free, suitable for resorts, tourist areas, golf courses, urban areas and factories with large premises. To make the vehicles perform effectively, the correct number of seats, battery type and configuration must be selected based on the actual route rather than only comparing the purchase price. ICD advises step by step, from route survey to vehicle delivery and battery charging instructions.

Choose the number of seats based on the area scale

Seats Suitable for Advantages Considerations
2 to 4 seats (golf carts, personal vehicles) Golf courses, villas, small resorts Compact, flexible, easy to turn around Small capacity, need more vehicles at peak times
6 seats Boutique resorts, hotels, small urban areas Balance between flexibility and capacity May lack seats when picking up groups
8 seats Resorts, medium tourist areas, group and family shuttle Popular configuration, reduces number of trips Need wide enough internal roads at curves
10 to 14 seats (mini bus type) Large tourist areas, historical sites, parks, fixed routes Reduces number of trips at peak hours Large size, requires suitable turning radius and slope

An effective approach is to combine: small vehicles for individual guests with flexible routes, and large vehicles for groups or fixed routes. ICD can simulate the number of vehicles needed based on the number of rooms, peak guest volume, and route length.

How electric tourist vehicles operate: battery, range, terrain

  1. Number of passengers per tripDetermine the seating capacity and number of vehicles needed for peak hours
  2. Daily travel distanceDetermines battery capacity, including when operating at full load
  3. TerrainReport the actual gradient percentage to select motor power and brakes
  4. Battery typeLead-acid or lithium battery depending on usage intensity
  5. ChargingArrange dry, safe charging points; typically charge overnight
The right question when choosing a vehicle

Do not just ask how far the vehicle can go on a single charge. Ask how far the vehicle can go when carrying a full load of passengers and operating on your specific terrain. For the same vehicle, the actual range on uphill roads with a full load is significantly lower than on flat roads with a light load.

Lead-acid battery or lithium battery for electric tourist vehicles?

Criteria Lead-acid battery Lithium battery
Initial cost Lower Higher
Charging time Long, often overnight charging Faster, more flexible
Weight Heavy Lighter
Lifespan Shorter Longer
Suitable for Moderate usage, easy overnight charging Multi-shift operation, needs constant readiness

Enclosed or open cabin, and legal considerations

Open cabin vehicles are airy, convenient for sightseeing and boarding, suitable for coastal resorts and eco-zones. Enclosed cabin vehicles or those with a roof and rain curtains are suitable for rainy areas or when air conditioning is needed. Many vehicles allow the roof and curtains to be removed and installed flexibly according to the season.

Legally, vehicles that only operate within private premises usually have simpler procedures than those that go on public roads. If the vehicle route goes through public roads or inter-zone roads, confirm the registration, inspection, and speed limit regulations with the local management authority before putting it into operation. ICD provides the vehicle’s technical documentation to support this.

Frequently Asked Questions about Passenger Electric Vehicles

How many seats should a resort choose for its electric vehicles?

Depending on the area, number of rooms and peak guest volume. 8-seat vehicles are a common configuration for medium resorts; small resorts can use 4 to 6-seat vehicles; large resorts should also have 10 to 14-seat vehicles for groups.

Can electric vehicles climb hills?

Yes, if you choose the right motor power. Let ICD know the steepest gradient on your route to avoid buying an underpowered configuration.

How long does charging take, and what about electricity costs?

Charging time depends on the battery type and charger, usually charging overnight to be ready the next morning. Electricity costs are significantly lower than gasoline, with details depending on usage.

Do electric tourist vehicles require registration and inspection?

It depends on the operating range. Within private premises it is usually simpler; going on public roads requires compliance with local regulations. Confirm with the management authority before operation.
