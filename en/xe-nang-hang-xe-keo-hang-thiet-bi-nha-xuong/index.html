Forklifts: advice on choosing a truck by load and warehouse layout

Electric forklifts, hand pallet trucks, and order pickers for warehouses and factories. ICD advises on configuration based on load capacity, lifting height, and aisle width.

Forklifts are equipment for lifting, loading, unloading, and transporting pallets in warehouses, factories, and distribution centers. Choosing the right type from the start helps maximize storage space, avoid overloading, and reduce operating costs. ICD offers electric forklifts, pallet trucks, reach trucks, and order pickers, along with configuration advice based on your warehouse’s actual conditions.

Common types of forklifts and when to use them

Type Features Best for
Electric pallet truck (powered pallet truck) Low-lift pallet handling, with electric motor for travel Transporting pallets over long distances in warehouses, supermarkets, packaging areas
Stacker Both moves and lifts to height (usually a few meters), compact size Small to medium warehouses, narrow aisles, stacking on low shelves
Reach truck Forks extend deep into racks, high lift height High-bay warehouses, narrow aisles, logistics centers
Counterbalance forklift (sit-down) Most common, 3 or 4 wheels, long shift operation Loading/unloading trucks and containers, moving pallets in factories
Order picker Operator rises with platform, picks individual items from racks Piece-picking warehouses, e-commerce, spare parts

There are also diesel forklifts for outdoor work, uneven surfaces, or very heavy loads. In enclosed spaces, food warehouses, pharmaceutical warehouses, and cold storage, electric forklifts are often the more suitable choice because they produce no emissions and operate quietly.

5 parameters to finalize before choosing a forklift

  1. Load capacityCalculate the heaviest load plus pallet; choose a forklift with reasonable margin
  2. Lift heightMeasure the highest rack beam, add safe clearance when placing the load
  3. Load centerDistance from the fork face to the load center, typically standard 500 or 600 mm
  4. Aisle widthMeasure the narrowest aisle and turning space to choose a stand-up or sit-down forklift
  5. Power sourceChoose lead-acid or lithium battery based on number of shifts and charging time
Read the load capacity specifications correctly

The load capacity stated on the forklift label is only valid at a specific load center and height. When lifting higher or with an off-center load, the safe lifting capacity decreases. Always check the load chart at the exact lift height you need, not just the number on the label.

Lithium battery or lead-acid battery?

Criteria Lead-Acid Battery Lithium Battery
Initial investment cost Lower Higher
Charging time Longer, typically requires scheduled charging Fast charging, can be topped up between shifts
Maintenance Requires water refilling, terminal cleaning Low maintenance
Battery lifespan Shorter Longer, often offsets initial cost for multi-shift operations
Suitability Single-shift operation, limited budget Continuous multi-shift operation, need to reduce downtime

Frequently asked questions about forklifts

Should I buy an electric forklift or a diesel forklift?

For indoor spaces or food and pharmaceutical warehouses, electric forklifts are more suitable because they produce no exhaust fumes and are quieter. Diesel forklifts are better for outdoor use, rough terrain, or very heavy loads. If both environments are involved, discuss with ICD for a combined solution.

How much extra load capacity should a forklift be chosen with?

There is no fixed number. Calculate the heaviest load you regularly lift, then check the load chart at the actual lift height and add a reasonable safety margin. ICD supports this calculation during the survey.

Which forklift should be used for narrow aisles?

Consider a stand-on reach truck or stacker because their turning radius is smaller than that of a sit-down counterbalance forklift. Measure the narrowest aisle to choose correctly.
