Market Overview

Plastic pallets, made from durable plastic materials such as HDPE, are essential transport structures in modern supply chains. Compared to wooden pallets, plastic pallets have a longer lifespan, are reusable many times, and pose less risk of contamination or cross-contamination, especially in industries with high hygiene standards such as pharmaceuticals and food. In 2023, the Pallet Pooling segment held a significant market share globally, and forecasts indicate that this segment will continue to grow strongly in the 2024-2031 period.

Plastic pallet pooling services allow companies to share and reuse pallets within a logistics network, reducing operational costs and optimizing logistics processes. This aligns with the sustainable development strategies of many businesses today as they strive to reduce carbon emissions and protect natural resources.

Plastic pallets, with their ability to be reused multiple times without harming the environment, have emerged as an ideal choice to meet these requirements. From large manufacturers to small businesses, the adoption of plastic pallets is helping to reduce waste and optimize supply chain operations.
  2. The Boom of E-commerce: The e-commerce industry is growing rapidly as global consumers shift to online shopping. The combination of plastic pallets and automation technology not only reduces errors but also improves real-time supply chain management and tracking.

Choosing a capacity by location

Location Suggested capacity Reason
Offices, small shops 60L - 120L Low waste volume, compact to keep aisles clear

This is a starting suggestion. Actual waste volume and collection frequency should decide the final choice.

How to calculate the capacity you need

Instead of choosing by habit, estimate with this formula: required capacity = daily waste (litres) x days between collections x 1.2. The 1.2 factor is a buffer so bins do not overflow on busy days.

Step Illustrative example (assumed)
1. Estimate daily waste 600 litres per day at one collection point
2. Set the collection frequency Daily, meaning 1 day between collections
3. Apply the formula 600 x 1 x 1.2 = 720 litres
4. Choose bins 2 bins of 660L (1320 litres) or 3 bins of 240L (720 litres)

The 600 litre figure is for illustration only. For a real site, measure actual waste over 5 to 7 days and use the highest day as the basis.

Four checks before a bulk order

  • Space and access: measure doors, lifts, corridors and the turning radius of wheeled bins. A 660L bin is over 1.3 m wide, so many narrow routes cannot take it.
  • Truck compatibility: 660L and 1000L bins usually follow the EN 840 standard for size and lifting mechanism. Ask the collection operator which lifting type they use before choosing a model.
  • Material: HDPE is light and easy to clean, suited to residential areas. Composite handles heat and impact better, suited to factories and industrial zones.
  • Accessories: lid, wheel locks, base drain plug and colours that follow local sorting rules.

Many people now use organic compost bins to turn kitchen and garden waste into well-rotted compost that feeds gardens and farmland. So what is an organic compost bin, and how do you make your own? Read on to find out.

What is an organic compost bin?

An organic compost bin is an important tool for processing waste and producing organic fertilizer. It is made of virgin HDPE plastic, which withstands high temperatures and is not affected by the surrounding environment. Because it is made from virgin resin, the product is both environmentally friendly and safe for users. The body usually has round holes about 10 - 15 cm apart, plus a door at the bottom for taking out the finished compost easily.

You can make your own compost bin for the following types of waste:

  • Chất thải trong nông nghiệp: rơm, rạ, các bộ phận như thân cây, cành cây, lá cây; các loại cỏ…
  • Chất thải trong công nghiệp: Vỏ cà phê, bã trà, bã mía, vỏ lạc,…
  • Các loại phế liệu từ giấy, sợi (trong nhà máy)
  • Đồ ăn thừa, thực phẩm hỏng: rau củ quả, thịt, cá, trứng,…
  • Chất thải sinh hoạt: Vải, sợi bông,…

Materials you need

To make an organic compost bin at home, you only need a few simple materials:

  • The bin: a plastic, foam or wooden container will do. Plastic is usually preferred for its durability, as it does not crack or fade over time.
  • A drill: used to make small holes in the bin so that air can circulate and moisture can escape during composting.
  • Composting materials: plant waste such as dry leaves, leftover vegetables and fruit, and plant-based food scraps. Avoid materials that are hard to break down or likely to smell, such as animal bones, quicklime and manure, to keep the compost quality high.

Why make a compost bin at home

Have you noticed that waste left in the house for too long starts to smell? Inorganic waste has no smell by itself; only organic waste that is mixed in or left in the bin for a long time does. Handled properly, the air in your home stays fresher and cleaner. Knowing how to make a compost bin therefore has several benefits:

  • Động vật nuôi sẽ ngừng bới thùng rác tìm thức ăn, rác sẽ ít vương vãi. Đồng thời, các loại ruồi, muỗi và côn trùng sẽ bớt dần. Nhờ đó ta hạn chế được sự sinh sôi của các loại bệnh truyền nhiễm do côn trùng mang theo.
  • Khi tận dụng nguồn rác thải hữu cơ ngoài môi trường hay trong sinh hoạt để ủ phân bón, bạn sẽ giúp giảm thiểu lượng rác thải ra môi trường.
  • Tự làm phân bón hữu cơ tại nhà giúp tiết kiệm được chi phí hơn thay vì mua bên ngoài.
  • Có nguồn dinh dưỡng bón cho cây trồng trong nhà.
  • Sử dụng phân hữu cơ giúp làm hạn chế sử dụng phân hóa học, bảo vệ môi trường và sức khỏe gia đình hơn.

How to make an organic compost bin

Making your own compost bin saves money, benefits your garden and helps the environment at the same time. The very simple guide below will help you make one at home quickly and effectively.

Step 1. Choose a bin

You can design your own or buy a plastic bin with a tight lid, about 60 cm tall or more. The lid keeps moisture in the soil and stops creatures from getting out.

Step 2. Drill holes in the bin

Use a drill or a sharp object to make 8 - 10 small holes in the bin so that air can circulate.

Step 3. Add a base layer

Put some shredded newspaper or dry leaves at the bottom of the bin, filling about 1/8 to 1/4 of it. These materials are easy for anyone to find, and using them also reuses items that would otherwise be thrown away.

Step 4. Add soil and dirt

Spread soil and dirt over the layer of leaves or newspaper until the bin is half full.

Step 5. Add food scraps

Now add any vegetables, leftovers or other products you want to compost. Compost only plant-based items, and avoid bones, quicklime and animal manure, which can breed harmful bacteria. Materials such as eggshells are especially good for compost.

Step 6. Stir the bin

Use a shovel or stick to mix the contents a little, making sure the food scraps are covered with soil.

Step 7. Moisten the bin

Spray with warm water until damp, but do not let the bin get too wet. Too much water can make the compost smell.

Step 8. Drill holes in the lid

Drill 8 - 10 small holes in the lid, then place a weight on top to hold it on the bin. The holes let air circulate inside.

Step 9. Find a place for the bin

Put the bin in a cool, shaded spot away from the house. If you live in an apartment or have no backyard, you can keep it on a porch or balcony. Avoid a spot with a lot of direct sunlight, or the compost will dry out and the process will be ruined.

Composting can produce bad smells and leaking liquid, and decomposing organic matter attracts insects. Both can seriously disturb daily life at home, so choose a spot far from living areas.

Step 10. Collect the decomposing material

The compost bin is now finished. Add leftover food when you have it and turn everything over each time; mixing well helps organic waste break down faster. To help collect materials for composting, you can also keep a container under the kitchen sink.

Now you only need to wait 2 - 3 months before the compost is ready to use. It can be used as mulch or potting soil, or sprinkled on the lawn as a conditioner. Keep at least 1/3 of the finished compost to start and sustain the next batch.

Basic principles of composting organic waste

Composting relies on biological decomposition by microorganisms. With enough oxygen, moisture and a balance of green and brown waste, microorganisms break the waste down into organic humus. The process can be aerobic or anaerobic; aerobic composting is usually faster and smells less. The result is nutrient-rich compost for plants.

What organic waste to compost and what to avoid

To limit unpleasant smells and keep the finished compost good, the choice of input material matters. Not all organic waste is suitable for composting. Note which types to use and which to avoid:

What to compost

Plant-based organic waste usually breaks down easily and smells little, for example:

  • Brown waste: dry leaves, shredded paper, cardboard, straw, dry grass, dry twigs, used tea bags, eggshells, sawdust and so on.
  • Green waste: fresh vegetable and fruit peels, freshly cut grass, coffee grounds, leaves, young twigs pruned from ornamental plants and so on.

What to avoid

Avoid materials that pollute or smell strongly, such as meat, fish, bones, egg yolk, dairy products, human or animal waste, treated wood and grease. Also avoid plants containing essential oils (such as lemongrass, mint, orange, mandarin and pine resin), because the oils hinder microorganisms and slow decomposition.

How to use and store compost, and how long it takes

Composting at home usually takes 30 to 60 days, depending on the materials and technique. To speed things up, many people use hot composting, which combines aerobic and anaerobic processes and can shorten the time to about 15 - 20 days.

In the first 7 days the waste starts to ferment, and the liquid can be used to water plants. After about 25 days, if the waste has broken down into a crumbly form, it can be used on plants. If not, keep adding a microbial preparation. When the compost is black, loose and odorless, it is ready to harvest.

To keep the compost in good condition after composting, follow these principles:

  • Store in a dry, airy place: keep the compost in a low-humidity, well-ventilated area to limit mold and harmful microorganisms.
  • Avoid direct sunlight: cover the compost or keep it in the shade so its quality is not reduced by the sun.
  • Keep away from chemicals: never let the compost touch pesticides or toxic chemicals, which can change its composition and harm plants.

Conclusion

Every small action can have a big impact. Recycling organic waste improves your home, the look of the city, your family’s health and, above all, the environment. What should a guard booth be made of to stay durable and cost-effective? Read on for a detailed answer.

Guard booths are commonly installed at offices, industrial parks and construction sites to control access. The market offers many booth types in different sizes, materials and colors. Which material should you choose? What is the standard size of a guard booth? Read this article for details.

Choosing booth material and size for durability, good looks and savings

Factors to consider when choosing guard booth material

Choosing the right material is critical when designing a guard booth. It directly affects durability, appearance and how well the booth works. Here are the key factors to consider.

Factors to consider for guard booth material

  • Durability and weather resistance: Booths usually stand outdoors, so the material must withstand sun, rain, wind and other environmental effects. Popular choices are stainless steel, galvanized steel, aluminium composite panel and fiberglass composite (FRP). They share strength, corrosion resistance, load bearing and heat tolerance, ensuring a long service life.
  • Sound and heat insulation: In noisy places such as beside main roads, factories or construction sites, sound insulation is essential. Heat insulation also keeps the guard comfortable in hot weather. Prefer materials with insulating foam, panel walls or double glazing for a pleasant working environment.
  • Safety and security: The booth must protect the staff inside and deter intruders. The material must be sturdy and able to carry security devices such as cameras, alarms and door locks. Tempered glass windows also improve security.
  • Appearance and architectural harmony: The booth should blend with its surroundings, such as urban areas, resorts, factories or office buildings. Aluminium-glass or wood-look composite not only look good but also give a professional impression consistent with the overall architecture.
  • Easy to build, transport and maintain: Flexibility in installation and maintenance matters when choosing material. Prefer light materials that are easy to transport and install, such as composite or aluminium. The material should also be easy to clean and little affected by weather to keep long-term maintenance costs low.
  • Investment cost: Choose according to your budget and purpose. Common materials such as composite and insulated sheet metal are moderately priced and suit short-term projects. Stainless steel and aluminium-glass cost more but offer better durability and appearance, suiting long-term projects with high image requirements.

Which material should a guard booth be made of?

Guard booths are usually placed outdoors. To stay durable and resist harsh weather, material choice is very important. By production material, guard booths fall into two main types: stainless steel booths and steel-frame booths clad with alloy panels.

Which material should a guard booth be made of

Stainless steel guard booths

This booth has a high-grade rust-free stainless steel frame, a non-slip aluminium floor and clear glass doors for easy observation. It comes with accessories such as power sockets, concealed wiring and switches. The width and height can be custom made.

The main advantage of this type is remarkable durability against weather and the environment. Stainless steel also improves the look of the interior.

However, stainless steel booths are usually fixed in place, which makes relocation difficult.

Stainless steel guard booth

Steel-frame guard booths clad with alloy

This booth is made of a stainless steel frame covered with aluminium alloy panels. Accessories include switches, sockets, a simple desk and more.

This model is compact, so it is easy to find a place for and easy to move. Designs and colors are varied, with different price levels to suit your taste and budget.

Despite these advantages, such booths are usually less luxurious than stainless steel ones and offer noticeably fewer amenities.

High-grade composite guard booths (FRP, WPC…)

Booths made of high-grade composite such as FRP (fiberglass-reinforced plastic) or WPC (wood-plastic composite) are valued for corrosion resistance, waterproofing and heat insulation. They are ideal for harsh environments such as coastal areas, industrial zones or high-humidity areas. The material is light, durable, termite-proof and easy to install and move. Composite can also be shaped flexibly to fit styles from modern to antique.

Aluminium frame with tempered glass guard booths

Aluminium-frame booths with tempered glass look modern and luxurious, suiting office buildings, upscale urban areas and shopping centers. The aluminium frame is light yet strong and does not rust, while tempered glass gives good visibility and high safety. The design also keeps the interior bright and professional, and makes it easy to add cameras, lights, air conditioning and more.

Soundproof galvanized steel guard booths

Galvanized steel is popular for its outstanding rigidity, load capacity and long life. With an inner soundproofing layer such as mineral wool or PU foam, the booth becomes a quiet workspace suited to noisy places such as construction sites, factories or toll stations. The galvanized coating also resists rust, is easy to maintain and performs steadily in harsh weather.

Soundproof galvanized steel guard booth

Galvanized or cold-rolled sheet guard booths

Booths made of galvanized or cold-rolled sheet have reasonable cost, fast construction and high durability. Cold-rolled sheet has an insulating layer that lowers interior temperature in the sun, while galvanized sheet resists rust in humid environments. This type suits industrial parks, factories, parking lots and projects that need to save cost while keeping basic functions.

Galvanized or cold-rolled sheet guard booth

Engineered wood or natural wood guard booths

For projects with high aesthetic requirements and a unified architecture, booths of engineered or natural wood are a refined choice for resorts, upscale retreats or villas. Natural wood gives a rustic, luxurious look, while properly treated engineered wood resists warping and termites. Wood needs regular maintenance to keep its durability and looks over time.

Engineered wood or natural wood guard booth

Suitable spaces for each type of guard booth

The right booth depends not only on material but also on the specifics of each space. Below are design suggestions for each type of location to get the best use and appearance.

Residential areas, apartment buildings, new urban areas

In modern residential and urban areas, a guard booth is not only the security staff workplace but also contributes to a professional, tidy landscape. Choose refined designs made of aluminium-glass, wood-look composite or powder-coated steel frames that serve their function and blend with the surrounding architecture.

Factories, industrial parks

In harsh environments such as factories or industrial parks, durability and load capacity come first. Booths here should be made of galvanized steel, insulated sheet metal or FRP composite, materials that resist corrosion and noise and withstand continuous operation. Also pay attention to sound insulation and ventilation to protect the health of the users.

Resorts and retreats

In upscale spaces such as resorts, a guard booth provides security and is also part of the landscape design. Booths should be made of natural wood, wood-plastic or wood-grain composite for a friendly, welcoming and luxurious feel. The design should match the overall architecture, blend with greenery and natural light, and can be customized for each area of the resort.

Schools and hospitals

In places with many people and special security needs, such as schools and hospitals, booths should use tempered glass for easy observation and quick response. Aluminium or galvanized steel frames are preferred for durability, safety, easy cleaning and suitability for public environments. Pay attention to the interior so staff on long shifts are comfortable.

Temporary or mobile sites

On temporary construction sites or at places that move often, booths should be made of light materials such as composite, galvanized sheet or aluminium frames for easy assembly and transport. The design should be simple and compact yet keep basic features such as a roof, ventilation and secure locks, and be easy to customize to the actual site.
